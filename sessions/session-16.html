Regression analysis

How does one variable change when another changes?

Regression analysis A statistical modelling technique that examines the relationship between a dependent variable (the target) and one or more independent variables (predictors). It quantifies how variables are related, predicts future outcomes, and helps explain relationships.

It answers questions like: how do sales depend on advertising spend? How does a house price depend on location, area and number of rooms?

The mathematical form

Y = W₀ + W₁X + ε

Y is the dependent variable, X the independent variable, W₀ the intercept (the value of Y when X = 0), W₁ the slope (how much Y changes for each one-unit increase in X), and ε the random error. The usual way to choose W₀ and W₁ is least squares: pick the line that makes the sum of squared errors (vertical distances from points to the line) as small as possible.

Measuring a regression model

Is the model any good, and which predictors matter?

MeasureMeaning
R² (coefficient of determination)Share of the variance in Y explained by the model, from 0 to 1
Adjusted R²R² penalized for the number of predictors, so useless predictors lower it
p-valueTests whether each predictor is statistically significant
F-statisticTests whether the model as a whole is significant
RMSEThe typical size of a prediction error, in Y's units
R² = 1 − SSE ÷ SST     Adjusted R² = 1 − (1 − R²)(n − 1) ÷ (n − k − 1)

SSE is the sum of squared errors, SST the total sum of squares around the mean of Y, n the number of observations and k the number of predictors.

Assumptions and limitations

When can you trust a linear regression?

Linearity

The relationship between X and Y is linear.

Independence

Observations are independent of one another.

Homoscedasticity

Errors have constant variance across X.

Normal errors

Errors are normally distributed.

No multicollinearity

Predictors aren't highly correlated with each other.

Diagnose a regression with residual plots

Interactive

A residual is actual minus predicted. If the assumptions hold, residuals scatter randomly around zero. Pick a dataset to see what each kind of violation looks like.

Advantages

Easy to interpret; gives both prediction and explanation; quantifies the strength and direction of relationships; the foundation for many machine learning models.

Limitations

Sensitive to outliers; assumes linear relationships; misses interactions unless they're added; and correlation isn't causation, so interpret coefficients carefully.

Regression gives the predictive insight Ŷ = f(X₁, X₂, …, Xₙ): an equation you can use, not just a measure of association.

What forecasting is

Estimating the future from the past

Forecasting Estimating future values of a variable, such as sales, demand, revenue or stock prices, from historical data and analytical models. Forecasting = historical data + analytical model + future estimation.

It helps organizations plan ahead, allocate resources and reduce uncertainty: predicting demand, planning production, inventory and staff, supporting strategy, anticipating market trends and risks, and budgeting.

TypeDescriptionExample
QualitativeExpert opinion, intuition and market knowledge; no historical data neededForecasting a new product
QuantitativeMathematical and statistical models of historical dataTime series forecasting, regression

Qualitative forecasting

When there's no history to learn from

Qualitative methods rely on human judgment. They are used when historical data is limited or missing: launching new products, entering new markets, or facing situations with no precedent.

Delphi methodExperts give anonymous estimates and refine them over several roundsFuture technology adoption

Sales force compositeSalespeople estimate their customers' demandRegional sales
Scenario buildingBest-case, worst-case and other futuresStrategic planning under uncertainty

Quantitative forecasting

Time series models and causal models

Time series models

Used when future values depend mainly on past patterns, such as sales, stock prices or energy use.

TechniqueDescriptionApplication
Naive forecastNext period = the last period's valueA quick baseline
Moving averageAverage of the last n periodsStable demand
Weighted moving averageMore weight on recent periodsResponds faster to change
Exponential smoothingExponentially decreasing weights on the pastSales, weather
Trend projectionA regression line through time, extended forwardLong-term demand trends
ARIMAA statistical model that captures trend and autocorrelation (SARIMA adds seasonality)Economic and financial forecasting
Seasonal decompositionSplits a series into trend, seasonality and noiseMonthly or quarterly patterns
Simple exponential smoothing: Ft+1 = αYt + (1 − α)Ft

Ft+1 is the forecast for the next period, Yt the actual value this period, Ft this period's forecast, and α (between 0 and 1) the smoothing constant: higher α reacts faster to recent changes.

Advantages

Reduces uncertainty, supports resource and budget planning, improves operational efficiency, and enables proactive strategy.

Limitations

Built on assumptions that can fail under sudden change; accuracy falls with longer horizons; depends heavily on data quality and model choice; external shocks (pandemics, policy changes) distort outcomes.

Session 16 in summary

  • Regression models Y = W₀ + W₁X + ε; least squares picks the line with the smallest sum of squared errors.
  • Judge a model with R², adjusted R², p-values, the F-test and RMSE; adjusted R² guards against useless predictors.
  • Check the assumptions with residual plots: linearity, independence, constant variance, normal errors, no multicollinearity.
  • Forecasting uses judgment (qualitative) when data is scarce, and time series or causal models when history is available.
  • Evaluate forecasts on data the model hasn't seen, with MSE, RMSE, MAPE and bias. Good forecasting = right data + right model + continuous monitoring.
