Exploring data

Getting to know a dataset before modeling it

Data exploration and visualization are the first and most important steps in any analysis. They help analysts understand patterns, detect anomalies, test hypotheses and communicate findings. Before building complex models, analysts explore the data to understand its structure, trends and relationships.

Objectives of data exploration

  1. Understand the structure

    How many variables there are, and what type each one is.

  2. Detect problems

    Find missing values, outliers and errors.

  3. Identify patterns

    Look at distributions and relationships between variables.

  4. Generate questions

    Form hypotheses for deeper analysis.

  5. Guide modeling

    Inform which features to select and how to build models.

What is data visualization

Making data easier to see, understand and share

Data visualization Representing data graphically so information is easier to understand, interpret and communicate. It turns numbers or text into charts, graphs and dashboards.

Objectives

Simplify

Make complex data easier to understand.

Highlight

Show patterns, trends and outliers quickly.

Tell the story

Support data-driven storytelling for decisions.

Explore

Let people interact with data for deeper insight.

Exploration and visualization together

Understanding, then communicating

Exploration helps you understand what is in the data. Visualization helps you communicate what you discovered.

Together they form the foundation of intelligent data analysis, guiding data cleaning, feature engineering and model design.

Modern tools make it easier than ever to explore complex datasets, find hidden insights, and share them clearly with both technical and non-technical audiences.

Data-driven decision making

Deciding with evidence instead of instinct alone

In a competitive market, organizations rely on data-driven decision making (DDDM) to guide strategy, operations and innovation. Rather than depending only on intuition or past experience, businesses analyze real-time data to make decisions that are more accurate, objective and profitable.

Data-driven decision making Collecting, analyzing and interpreting data to guide strategic and operational business actions.

Seven key steps

  1. Define the business problem

    Identify the decision area, such as sales, customer retention or logistics.

  2. Collect relevant data

    Internal (sales records, transactions) or external (market trends, social media).

  3. Clean and prepare the data

    Ensure accuracy, remove duplicates and handle missing values.

  4. Analyze the data

    Apply statistics, predictive modeling or visualization tools.

  5. Interpret the results

    Translate patterns and metrics into business insight.

  6. Implement decisions

    Take action guided by the analysis.

  7. Evaluate outcomes

    Measure the impact and refine the strategy if needed.

Five company case studies

Making the right business decisions based on data

Compare how five companies used data

Browse

Amazon: personalized recommendations

Background. Amazon serves millions of customers worldwide and wanted to increase sales and engagement.
Business problem. How to recommend relevant products to each customer to boost purchases and satisfaction.

Data used

  • Purchase history
  • Browsing patterns
  • Ratings and reviews
  • Clickstream data

Analysis and tools

  • Machine learning (collaborative filtering, neural networks)
  • Big data analytics on AWS, Hadoop and Spark

Decision and outcome

Amazon built a personalized recommendation engine, which improved customer satisfaction and retention significantly.

35–40%of total sales came from recommendations

Netflix: data-driven content and recommendations

Background. Netflix competes in a crowded streaming market and needed to keep subscribers and improve their viewing experience.
Business problem. How to recommend content people love, and decide which shows to produce next.

Data used

  • Viewing habits (time spent, likes and dislikes)
  • Device usage patterns
  • Demographics and search queries

Analysis and tools

  • Predictive analytics and AI models of user preference
  • A/B testing of personalized recommendations
  • Big data analytics with Apache Spark and Python (pandas, scikit-learn)

Decision and outcome

Netflix commissioned House of Cards and other originals based on viewing data, increasing engagement and subscription retention, and showing that data can guide creative decisions too.

Starbucks: location and menu optimization

Background. Starbucks needed to decide where to open new stores and how to shape its menu.
Business problem. How to choose profitable locations and tailor products to local tastes.

Data used

  • Demographics (income, population density)
  • Location analytics (traffic patterns, competitors)
  • Customer purchasing behavior

Analysis and tools

  • Geospatial analytics, predictive modeling and AI simulation
  • Tableau, Python, ArcGIS

Decision and outcome

Starbucks opened stores in predicted high-profit zones and launched region-specific menu items, improving sales forecasting and customer satisfaction.

Walmart: inventory and supply chain optimization

Background. Walmart runs one of the world's largest retail supply chains.
Business problem. How to predict demand accurately and keep inventory at the right level.

Data used

  • Sales and seasonal data
  • Weather forecasts
  • Local events and holidays

Analysis and tools

  • Predictive analytics with Hadoop, SAS and machine learning
  • Real-time monitoring with IoT sensors

Decision and outcome

Better inventory management and on-shelf availability, less waste and lower logistics costs, and happier customers thanks to data-driven stocking. Try the simulation below.

Uber: dynamic pricing

Background. Uber wanted to set prices based on demand, supply and location.
Business problem. How to balance driver availability with rider demand as it changes minute by minute.

Data used

  • GPS and traffic data
  • Rider and driver availability
  • Time of day and event information

Analysis and tools

  • Real-time analytics and AI-driven algorithms
  • Python, Spark, AWS

Decision and outcome

Uber introduced surge (dynamic) pricing, balancing driver supply with rider demand and increasing revenue and operational stability. Try the simulation below.

Lessons learned

What the five cases have in common

Data is an asset

Collecting and analyzing it well drives innovation.

The right tools matter

Tableau, Python, Spark and Power BI make insight scalable.

Visualization communicates

Dashboards make complex results simple to grasp.

Monitor continuously

Feedback loops improve decisions over time.

Human + data

Data supports human judgment; it doesn't replace it.

Session 4 in summary

Exploration reveals the quality, structure and relationships in data; visualization turns those findings into clear visuals that drive decisions.

Amazon, Netflix, Starbucks, Walmart and Uber show that combining reliable data, modern tools and careful interpretation makes decisions smarter, faster and more profitable.
