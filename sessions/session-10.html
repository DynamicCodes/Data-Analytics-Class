What feature selection is

Keeping the inputs that matter

Feature selection The process of identifying the most relevant and significant features (independent variables) that contribute to predicting the target variable. Statistical hypothesis tests such as the Z-test, t-test, ANOVA and chi-square are often used in filter-based feature selection to check whether a feature is significantly related to the target.

Fewer, better features make models faster to train, easier to interpret and less likely to overfit.

A formal procedure

  1. Define the objective

    Specify the prediction task, the target variable and the problem type: classifying churn, predicting a stock price, forecasting temperature.

  2. Prepare the data

    Handle missing values, outliers and noise; encode categorical variables; normalize or scale numerical features.

  3. Identify feature and target types

    Numerical/continuous, categorical/discrete, or binary/ordinal. The types decide which statistical test to use.

  4. Apply feature selection techniques

    Filter, wrapper or embedded methods; for filters, run the appropriate test and rank features by p-value or score.

  5. Evaluate model performance

    Train on the reduced feature set, compare metrics (accuracy, RMSE, F1-score) and check for overfitting or underfitting.

  6. Finalize the feature subset

    Choose the smallest subset with the best generalization performance and interpretability.

Filter methods

Rank features with statistics, before any model is trained

Filter methods rely on statistical tests and properties of the data, independent of any model.

TechniqueWhat it doesExample
Correlation testKeep features highly correlated with the target but not with each otherPearson / Spearman correlation
Chi-square testTests independence between a categorical feature and the targetscipy.stats.chi2_contingency()
ANOVA F-testCompares a numeric feature's mean across target classesf_classif (scikit-learn)
Mutual informationMeasures dependency between feature and target using entropymutual_info_classif()

Advantages

Fast, simple, and independent of the model.

Limitations

Looks at one feature at a time, so it ignores interactions between features.

Wrapper methods

Let a model judge each subset of features

Wrapper methods use a predictive model to evaluate feature subsets, searching for the combination that performs best.

TechniqueHow it worksExample
Forward selectionStart with no features and add one at a time until there's no improvementRecursive search
Backward eliminationStart with all features and remove the least significant one at a timeStepwise regression
Recursive feature elimination (RFE)Repeatedly drop the least important features according to model weightssklearn.feature_selection.RFE

Advantages

Accounts for feature interactions; driven by the actual model.

Limitations

Computationally expensive, and can overfit on small datasets.

Practical tip: use filter methods first to remove obviously irrelevant features, then wrapper or embedded methods for fine-grained selection, and finally validate with cross-validation or a held-out test set.

Hypothesis testing

The engine behind filter methods

Hypothesis testing uses sample data to make decisions about a population parameter. You start with two opposing statements, then use the data to decide whether to reject or fail to reject the null hypothesis.

TypeSymbolMeaning
Null hypothesisH₀No effect, no difference, the status quo
Alternative hypothesisH₁ or HₐThere is an effect or difference
TermMeaning
PopulationThe entire set, such as all customers
SampleThe subset used for testing
ParameterA numerical property of the population, such as its mean or variance
StatisticA value computed from the sample
Significance level αThe threshold for rejecting H₀, often 0.05

Steps

  1. State the hypotheses

    H₀: μ = μ₀ (no difference); H₁: μ ≠ μ₀ (a difference).

  2. Set the significance level

    Commonly α = 0.05.

  3. Choose the test statistic

    Z-test when the population variance is known or the sample is large; t-test when the variance is unknown or the sample small; chi-square for categorical data; ANOVA to compare more than two groups.

  4. Compute the p-value

    The probability of data at least this extreme if H₀ is true.

  5. Apply the decision rule

    If p ≤ α, reject H₀; if p > α, fail to reject H₀.

TestPurposeExample
Z-testCompare a sample mean to a population mean (large n, known σ)Churners' monthly bill vs. the company average
t-testCompare means when σ is unknown or n is smallTwo drugs' effects
Chi-square testCompare categorical frequenciesGender vs. product preference
ANOVA (F-test)Compare more than two group meansThree teaching methods
Correlation testRelationship between two variablesHeight vs. weight

The Z-test

Does a sample mean differ from a known population mean?

The Z-test compares a sample mean with a population mean when the population standard deviation σ is known and the sample is large (n > 30). It tells you whether the sample differs significantly from the population assumption.

Z = (X̄ − μ₀) ÷ (σ / √n)

X̄ is the sample mean, μ₀ the hypothesized population mean, σ the population standard deviation, and n the sample size.

Example

A telecom company's customers pay ₹700 a month on average, with σ = ₹150. A sample of 64 customers who churned paid an average of ₹742. Do churners pay differently from the typical customer?

Z = (742 − 700) ÷ (150 / √64) = 42 ÷ 18.75 = 2.24

For a two-tailed test at α = 0.05 the critical value is ±1.96. Since 2.24 > 1.96 (p ≈ 0.025), we reject H₀: churners pay significantly more. In feature selection terms, monthly charges is a feature worth keeping.

Session 10 in summary

  • Feature selection keeps the features that genuinely help prediction, following a procedure from objective to final subset.
  • Filter methods rank features with statistical tests (correlation, t-test/ANOVA, chi-square, mutual information); they are fast but ignore interactions.
  • Wrapper methods search subsets with a model (forward, backward, RFE); embedded methods select during training (LASSO, trees).
  • Hypothesis tests decide between H₀ and H₁ by comparing the p-value with α; the choice of test depends on the data types.
  • The Z-test, Z = (X̄ − μ₀) ÷ (σ/√n), compares a sample mean with a known population mean when σ is known and n is large.
