What descriptive statistics do

A snapshot summary of a dataset

Descriptive statistics Measures that summarize and organize data so it can be easily understood. They describe the main features of a dataset: its central tendency, dispersion and shape.

Central tendency

The typical value: mean, median, mode.

Dispersion

How spread out values are: range, variance, standard deviation, coefficient of variation.

Position

Where a value stands: percentiles, quartiles, interquartile range.

Shape

The pattern of the distribution: skewness and kurtosis.

They are the foundation of data analysis. Before any advanced statistical or predictive method, these measures show you the patterns, the variability and the overall distribution of your data.

Central tendency

The center, or typical value, of a dataset

Mean (average)

Mean = ΣXi ÷ n

Add all values and divide by how many there are. Sensitive to outliers: one extreme value can pull it a long way.

Median

Middle value of the sorted data

With an even count, take the average of the two middle values. Much less affected by extreme values.

Mode

Most frequent value(s)

There can be one, several, or none. The only center measure that works for categorical data.

Dispersion

How spread out the values are

Range

Range = Max − Min

Simple, but depends only on the two most extreme values, so it is sensitive to outliers.

Variance (σ²)

σ² = Σ(Xi − X̄)² ÷ n

The average squared distance from the mean. For a sample, divide by n − 1 instead (written s²).

Standard deviation (σ)

σ = √Variance

The typical distance of a value from the mean, in the same units as the data.

Coefficient of variation (CV)

CV = (SD ÷ Mean) × 100

Spread relative to the mean, as a percentage. Lets you compare variability across datasets with different units or scales.

Position: percentiles and quartiles

Where a value stands relative to the rest

Percentiles

Divide sorted data into 100 equal parts. The 90th percentile is the value below which 90% of observations fall.

Quartiles

Divide data into four equal parts: Q1 (25th percentile), Q2 (the median, 50th) and Q3 (75th).

Interquartile range (IQR)

IQR = Q3 − Q1

The spread of the middle 50% of the data. Unaffected by extreme values, and the basis of the box plot.

Shape: skewness and kurtosis

The pattern of the distribution

Skewness

Measures asymmetry.
Positive skew: a long tail on the right.
Negative skew: a long tail on the left.

Kurtosis

Measures peakedness or flatness.
Leptokurtic: tall and narrow.
Mesokurtic: normal shape.
Platykurtic: flat.

Application example

In a dataset of patients' ages, the mean is 45 years, the median 43 years and the standard deviation 10 years. The average patient is 45, and most patients are between 35 and 55 (within one standard deviation of the mean). The median sitting a little below the mean hints at a few older patients pulling the average up.

Business case: monthly sales

A time series analyzed with central tendency and dispersion

A retail company wants to analyze its sales for the last 12 months in order to:

  • understand its typical monthly sales (central tendency),
  • measure how stable, or risky, its sales are (dispersion),
  • support forecasting and decision making.

Step-by-step calculations

Mean ₹585,000
X̄ = ΣXi ÷ n = 7,020 ÷ 12 = 585

On average, the company earns ₹585,000 a month.

Median ₹585,000

Sorted: 480, 510, 520, 560, 570, 580, 590, 600, 610, 640, 660, 700

Median = (580 + 590) ÷ 2 = 585

The median equals the mean, so sales are roughly symmetric with no extreme outliers.

Mode None

No value repeats, so there is no mode. Each month's sales are different, consistent with steady growth rather than repeated plateaus.

Range ₹220,000
Range = Max − Min = 700 − 480 = 220

Sales varied by ₹220,000 across the year.

Variance and standard deviation σ ≈ ₹60,900

Deviations from the mean (Xi − 585): −105, −65, −75, −5, 15, −25, 25, 5, −15, 55, 75, 115

Squared and added: 11,025 + 4,225 + 5,625 + 25 + 225 + 625 + 625 + 25 + 225 + 3,025 + 5,625 + 13,225 = 44,500

σ² = 44,500 ÷ 12 = 3,708.33  ⇒  σ = √3,708.33 ≈ 60.9

Monthly sales typically sit about ₹61,000 away from the average.

If these 12 months are treated as a sample of a longer history, divide by n − 1 instead: s² = 44,500 ÷ 11 = 4,045.45 and s ≈ 63.6.

Coefficient of variation ≈ 10.4%
CV = (σ ÷ X̄) × 100 = (60.9 ÷ 585) × 100 ≈ 10.4%

Only about 10% variation relative to the mean: sales are relatively stable and predictable. (Using the sample SD, CV ≈ 10.9%.)

Quartiles and IQR IQR = ₹67,500
Q1 = 550  ·  Q2 = 585  ·  Q3 = 617.5  ⇒  IQR = 67.5

The middle half of months fell between ₹550,000 and ₹617,500. (Calculated with the same linear interpolation method as Excel's QUARTILE.INC; other methods give slightly different values.)

Reading the time series

  • Trend: a gradual increase, especially from June to December, showing good business momentum.
  • Low variability: stable demand with consistent growth means low business risk.
  • Forecasting: the upward trend and the November–December peak suggest seasonality, which a forecast for next year should account for.
